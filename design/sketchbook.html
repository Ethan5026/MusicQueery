MusicQueerysketchbook

A live lead sheet that follows whatever you play on Spotify. It shows the chord you're on, what's coming, the key you're in, the next key change, and what to play over it, transposed for piano, E♭ alto or B♭ tenor. It also plays its own backing tracks.

Before the screens

What the app knows, and where it comes from

Every screen below is driven by four kinds of data. Only the first comes from Spotify directly.

What you seeSourceHow exact
Track, play/pause, positionSpotify Web API (playback on any device) or the Spotify iOS SDK (playback on this phone)Exact
Polled about once a second and interpolated on the phone between polls. Target: within 150 ms of what you hear.
Chords, sections, key changesA chord timeline made by analysing the recording's audio, saved per Spotify track IDExact once analysed Live only on first listen
Look-ahead (Up next, key change countdown) needs a saved timeline.
Key, tempo, time signatureDerived from the same analysis, with a third-party lookup as a fallbackGood
Tempo and key per section, so rubato and modulations are handled.
Backing tracks (generated)The app's own band playing a chord chartExact
The chords are known before the first bar, so no analysis is needed and look-ahead always works.
Scales, functions, guide tonesOn-device theory engineComputed
Calculated from the chords and transposed for your instrument.

Spotify won't tell us the chords. Spotify closed its Audio Features and Audio Analysis endpoints (key, tempo, sections) to new apps in November 2024, and it has never offered chords. Getting a chord timeline is the hardest problem in this project, so the development plan opens with a spike on it. The sample data in these sketches stands in for that timeline.

    Read top to bottom, in order of urgency

    Press Play demo above to watch it move at the recording's real tempo, or drag the song slider. Switch Read as to see the sax views.

    Sync badge
    How closely the dashboard tracks Spotify's playhead. Green under 150 ms, amber while it re-locks after a seek or skip.
    Home and Now
    The tune's home key, and the key centre you're in right now. This tune passes through five of them.
    Current chord
    Lettered like a Real Book chart and sized to read from a music stand. The Roman numeral shows its job in the current key, and the dots count beats in the bar. The source tag shows whether the chords come from a saved analysis or are being heard live.
    Up next
    The next four changes with a beat countdown, so a ii–V never surprises you.
    Key change
    Counts down to the next modulation and turns rust-red for the last bar.
    Play
    The suggested scale on a treble staff, with a more colourful alternative named beside it. The current chord is stacked as whole notes at the start of the bar, then the scale ascends through the octave. Every accidental is written out, with no key signature. Brass noteheads are chord tones and blue ones are guide tones (3rd and 7th). The sax views show written pitch. Keys swaps the staff for the keyboard. The bottom line shows how the guide tones move into the next chord by the smallest step.

    Backing tracks

    Three sources, one Stand

    Try the three tabs on the phone. Key colours use the same circle-of-fifths scheme as the Song map.

    Generated
    The app's own piano, bass and drums play from a chord chart. The chords are exact from bar one, so Stand always has full look-ahead. Change the key, tempo and style, and mute your own instrument: piano off when you comp, everything on when you play sax. Importing iReal Pro playlists adds large community libraries of jazz standards.
    On Spotify
    Searches Spotify for play-along and minus-one recordings, including published play-along series where available. These play through Spotify and use the chord timeline like any song: exact once analysed.
    Drills
    Progressions through all 12 keys: major and minor ii–V–I, the blues, turnarounds, Coltrane changes, modal vamps, and a drone for long tones and intonation. All generated, so the chords are exact.
    Components and states

    When things aren't perfect

    The dashboard tells you how much to trust it. These badges appear in the same places on every screen.

    Sync with Spotify

    • ±40 msLocked. Position is interpolated between polls and corrected on every response.
    • Re-syncingAfter a seek, skip or network hiccup. The chart freezes on the last confirmed bar instead of guessing.
    • PausedSpotify is paused. The chart stays where you left it.
    • Not connectedSign in again. The button opens Spotify's login.

    Where the chords came from

    • AnalysedA saved timeline for this exact recording. Full look-ahead.
    • Listening liveChords detected as they play, about half a second behind. Up next is hidden.
    • PredictedInferred from repeats of the form. Shown in grey until the audio confirms it.

    Nothing playing

    N.C.

    Play something on Spotify, on this phone or any speaker, and the chart picks it up within a second.

    What a fresh session reads

    1. CLAUDE.md names the current stage and the house rules. About 30 lines.
    2. handoffs/DECISIONS.md lists frozen decisions with IDs, so they aren't argued again.
    3. The latest handoff covers what was built and verified, what's open, and the exact next step. 120 lines at most.

    Together that's a few thousand tokens. Older handoffs stay in the repo as history, and nobody needs to load them.

    Settled in round 1

    Your decisions

    1. Design direction: approved, with two additions. The scale and chord now appear on a treble staff, and there's a Backing tracks screen.
    2. Saxophone: both. The toggle offers alto and tenor, alto first.
    3. Chord source: a Windows companion analyses Spotify's audio on your PC, and the phone's mic covers everywhere else. Both share one cache per track. Generated backing tracks need neither.
    4. Build machine: Windows only. Expo/React Native with EAS cloud builds and TestFlight.
